# Rendering and PS2 grounding

Xavier's original crude CGI controls his mesh and materials. Original-PS2 references translate the world around him.

## Ground before generation

1. Resolve the exact original-PS2 build and region when relevant.
2. Find and visually inspect three to five authentic gameplay or in-engine screenshots.
3. Reject remasters, HD collections, later ports, emulator texture packs, widescreen hacks, mods, ReShade, fan art/renders, box art, promotional renders, and platform-ambiguous captures.
4. Choose one primary game and at most two secondary games.
5. Assign each accepted reference one narrow role.
6. Derive a scene-specific rendering contract.
7. Keep Xavier's canonical sheet above every game reference for identity and construction.
8. Record the inspected sources and roles in the response or project grounding log.

For a show-adjacent desert, official show frames control mood; a PS2 title may still control camera and render limits.

## Default contract

- 4:3 gameplay framing unless delivery needs a social crop
- 480i/640×480-era feel before resize
- chunky silhouettes and limited geometry
- 128–256-pixel-feeling diffuse textures on ordinary surfaces
- vertex/Gouraud-style or baked lighting
- flat materials and crude projected/blob shadows
- restrained fog/draw distance
- sparse particles and simple transparency
- mild aliasing, filtering, color banding, and capture softness

Reject PBR, ray tracing, volumetric shafts, cinematic bokeh, micro-surface detail, strand hair/fur, modern Unreal grading, remaster sharpness, or excessive VHS/scanline overlays used to conceal modern geometry. Repair the construction and light before adding capture artifacts.
